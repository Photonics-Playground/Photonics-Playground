WORDS YOU WILL MEET

A pocket glossary

The same definitions appear on dotted words throughout the tools. Hover or tap to read them.

Waveguide
A strip or layer of high-index material that carries light, like a wire carries current.
Refractive index n
How much slower light travels in a material than in vacuum. Glass ≈ 1.45, silicon ≈ 3.48.
Core and cladding
The high-index strip that carries light, and the lower-index material around it.
Total internal reflection
Complete reflection at a boundary to a lower-index material, beyond the critical angle.
Mode
A field pattern that travels along a waveguide without changing shape.
Effective index
How fast a mode travels along the guide, expressed as an index.
Evanescent tail
The part of a mode outside the core. It lets neighbouring waveguides exchange light.
TE and TM
The two polarizations: electric field sideways (TE) or up and down (TM).
Coupling length
How far two coupled waveguides must run for all light to cross over.
Resonance and FSR
Wavelengths that fit a whole number of times around a ring, and their spacing.
Scattering matrix
A small table that says how much light goes from each input to each output of a component.
Inverse design
Letting an optimizer find the shape that meets a goal, guided by gradients.
UNDER THE HOOD

Real solvers, running on your own device

Which solvers are used

  • Mode solver: finite-difference eigenmode solver for waveguide cross-sections (quasi-TE and quasi-TM).
  • 2D device simulation: frequency-domain finite-difference solver (FDFD) of the scalar wave equation, with absorbing boundaries and mode sources and monitors.
  • Inverse design: adjoint gradients with an Adam optimizer, density filtering and projection toward binary structures.

Everything runs locally

All computation happens in your browser, on your own processor. There is no server, no account and no upload: nothing you draw or simulate leaves your device. Once a page is loaded it also works offline.

The tools are built for intuition and teaching. The device simulations are 2D models, which capture guiding, interference and inverse design faithfully, but they do not replace full 3D simulation of a fabricated chip.
