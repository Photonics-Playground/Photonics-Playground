What to look for

TWO DIMENSIONS

From slab to channel

A slab waveguide confines light in one direction only. A wire (strip) waveguide also confines it sideways, so each mode now has two numbers: how many lobes across the width and across the height. Narrow the waveguide and watch higher modes slip below the cladding line.

POLARIZATION

TE and TM

Light is a wave whose electric field points in a certain direction, its polarization. On a chip there are two natural choices: the field points sideways, parallel to the chip surface (TE, transverse electric), or up and down (TM, transverse magnetic). The two see the core differently, so each has its own effective index and its own mode shape. Switch between TE and TM above to compare them. In a wire waveguide the field is mostly, not purely, in one direction, which is why the modes are called quasi-TE and quasi-TM.

BIREFRINGENCE

Geometry sets polarization

Make the core square and the TE and TM indices nearly meet. Thin, wide cores are strongly birefringent, which is why silicon photonics is typically designed for TE.

MODE BEATING

Interference along z

Two modes travel at different speeds, so their sum shifts sideways and back again with the beat length. This is the principle behind multimode interference couplers and directional couplers.

L_beat = λ / (n_eff,1 − n_eff,2)
