Grating Coupler Optimizer

A uniform grating coupler leaves a lot of light behind. Here the optimizer is free to place every trench of an 8 µm long grating, with one goal: get as much light as possible into the fiber.

Two things limit a uniform grating. It emits most of the light right at its start, decaying from there, while the fiber accepts a symmetric Gaussian spot. And it sends about a third of the light down into the substrate. An apodized grating, with trenches that change along the way, fixes the first problem. The second is set mainly by the layer stack: the oxide thickness and etch depth.

Press Optimize. Each step runs one simulation of light leaving the chip and one adjoint simulation, which tells every one of the 160 columns whether it should be silicon or etched. The design stays a single 100 nm etch, so it could be made in one lithography step.

Model and accuracy. 2D side-view (y–z) frequency-domain simulation, 50 nm grid with subpixel smoothing: oxide cladding, 220 nm silicon, 2 µm buried oxide, silicon substrate. Fiber coupling is the upward power multiplied by its overlap with a tilted Gaussian of 5.2 µm waist. Adjoint gradients checked against finite differences. The uniform reference is a 630 nm period, 50 % fill, 100 nm deep grating at the same fiber position and angle.
