Inverse Design 2D

Instead of drawing a component by hand, say what it should do and let an optimizer find the shape. Pick a device, press Optimize, and watch 961 pixels of core material rearrange themselves until the light goes where you want it: around a corner, into two ports, sorted by color, or from one waveguide mode into another.

What is happening

FORWARD SIMULATION

Solving Maxwell in 2D

The domain is a 69×69 grid with 80 nm pixels. Each iteration solves the frequency-domain wave equation directly (FDFD), with absorbing edges so light leaves cleanly. The source s launches the input mode.

∇²Efwd + k₀² ε(r) Efwd = −s
OBJECTIVE

Light in the right mode

The figure of merit (FOM) is the power coupled into a chosen mode of each output waveguide, divided by the input power. For the demultiplexer and the mode sorter, light reaching the wrong port is penalized.

FOM = |⟨φout, Efwd⟩|² / (κout · Pin)
ADJOINT

Two solves, 961 gradients

One extra simulation, the adjoint simulation with a source placed at the output, gives the sensitivity of every pixel at once. Switch the design view to Gradient to see where adding material helps. How Inverse Design Works derives it step by step.

∂FOM/∂εᵢ ∝ Re( Eadj,i · Efwd,i )
FABRICATION

Feature size and binarization

A blur filter sets the minimum feature size, and a sharpening projection pushes each pixel to core or cladding. Larger filters give smoother, more manufacturable shapes at a small cost in efficiency.

SYMMETRY

Built-in constraints

The splitter design is forced to be mirror-symmetric, which guarantees an exact 50/50 split. The mode converter needs the opposite: it must break the symmetry to turn the even TE₀ mode into the odd TE₁ mode.

SCALING UP

From toy to real devices

Real designs use 3D solvers like FDTD with millions of voxels on GPUs, but the loop is the same: simulate, run the adjoint, update, repeat.

Model and accuracy. 2D scalar model (E out of plane). Core n = 2.2, cladding n = 1.45, waveguide widths 400 nm (single-mode) and 880 nm (two-mode), design region 2.48 × 2.48 µm. Optimizer: Adam with cone filter and tanh projection. Runs entirely on your device. Accuracy: cross-checked against an independent solver with perfectly matched layers. On this coarse 80 nm grid, efficiencies are within about 3 percentage points of a converged 20 nm simulation, except for the color demultiplexer, whose wavelength selectivity makes it about 10 points optimistic.
